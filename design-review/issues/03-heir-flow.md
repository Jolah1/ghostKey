Title: UX: warm and layer the heir flow, our highest stakes user

Labels: ux, trust, heir, design

## Summary

The heir is the most vulnerable user in the product, often grieving and new to Bitcoin, yet they currently meet the coldest and most technical screens. These changes make the flow gentle without losing the depth a technical helper needs. The actual claim confirmation lives in issue 01 (L3).

## Items

### H1. The Inherit entry page is cold and its title can read as blame (TRUST, S)
"You should have received a link" can feel like "you did something wrong" to someone who has not got the message yet. Lead with reassurance: "If you are here, someone trusted you to look after something they left behind. We will walk you through it slowly." Keep the honest "no link yet, that's normal" note.
Done when: the page opens with a warm human line before any mechanics.

### H2. Token vocabulary on the least technical screen (TRUST, S)
"one-time access token" and "the bit after /claim/" are developer framing. Replace with "Your link is private and works only once. Please do not share it." Show the example link if helpful, but do not ask the heir to understand a token.
Done when: no token vocabulary remains on the heir entry page.

### H3. The emergency recovery file drops a non technical heir into descriptors (TRUST, M)
The top half is excellent plain education. The bottom half is the most jargon dense screen in the product (descriptors, Bitcoin Core, Sparrow, Liana, raw strings). Split it into a calm heir layer ("Type your password and press Unlock, that is all you need") and a clearly separated, collapsed layer headed "For a Bitcoin expert helping you" that holds the technical content. Lead that section with "any Bitcoin expert can help using just this file." Keep translating block counts to days.
Done when: the heir sees only password and Unlock by default; technical content is collapsed.

### H4. Consider defaulting the heir flow to the light theme (POLISH, M, decision)
GhostKey ships both themes, so neither is off brand. The heir is a different person, often on a different device, in a difficult emotional state, with no relationship to the darker brand. Defaulting the heir flow to the lighter, calmer theme could be a deliberate choice. The Muun exploration in this review shows how that feels.
Done when: a decision is made and documented.
